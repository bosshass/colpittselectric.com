Case study 01 · Public sector · Certified participation

Colorado Convention Center — Denver

A $300,000 electrical subcontract on a City and County of Denver project, delivered in full. Colpitts Electric was brought on by the electrical prime to meet Denver’s certified participation requirements — and supplied the crew to perform the work.

Project facts Tap to expand+
ProjectColorado Convention Center · Denver, Colorado
Contract chainHensel Phelps (general contractor) → Weifield Group (electrical prime) → Colpitts Electric (subcontractor)
Contract value$300,000 — fulfilled in full
Why we were brought onDenver SBE participation on a City and County of Denver project
Crew supplied4 journeyman electricians, 1 apprentice, 1 working foreman (Melissa Colpitts)
ScopeElectrical wire pulling
DatesJune 2025 – December 31, 2025

The situation

Public work in the City and County of Denver carries small business participation requirements. Primes bidding City projects have to show certified participation — and they need subs who are both certified and genuinely capable, because a certified sub who can’t perform costs the prime more than the participation credit is worth.

Colpitts Electric holds City and County of Denver SBE (Small Business Enterprise) certification, and was brought onto the Colorado Convention Center by Weifield Group, the electrical prime, on that basis.

What we did

Colpitts Electric supplied and supervised a six-person crew — four journeyman electricians, one apprentice, and a working foreman — pulling wire across the facility from June through the end of December 2025.

Melissa Colpitts ran the crew on site, coordinating daily with the electrical prime and the general contractor inside a live, high-profile public building.

Certified participation that performs

Colpitts Electric arrives with a licensed Master Electrician running the crew, journeyman electricians who can be turned loose, and a foreman who has held the superintendent seat on commercial jobs. The participation credit comes without a supervision burden.

An apprentice was carried on the crew for the duration — relevant on Colorado public work, where apprenticeship utilization requirements apply to qualifying projects.

The result

The full $300,000 contract was performed and completed on schedule, closing out at the end of December 2025.

Accuracy: this was a subcontract under the electrical prime, not a direct award from the City and County of Denver.

Apprenticeship

We can carry apprentices on public work.

Colorado public projects of $1 million or more must comply with the apprenticeship utilization requirements of C.R.S. §24-92-115, and the contractor has to submit apprenticeship certification for electrical work before the contract is executed. Only apprentices registered with a registered apprenticeship program count toward those requirements.

Colpitts Electric participates in registered apprenticeship program name sponsor / participant number, which means our apprentices attend related classroom instruction and accrue credited hours — and count toward apprenticeship utilization on qualifying public projects.

Verify before publishing: confirm the exact program name, that it is registered with the U.S. DOL Office of Apprenticeship or the Colorado State Apprenticeship Agency, and Colpitts’ standing as a participating employer. Do not claim apprenticeship utilization eligibility on an unregistered training arrangement.

Case study 02 · Commercial LED retrofit

426-Fixture LED Lighting Retrofit — BRAVE Church, Cherry Hills Village

Colpitts Electric holds the electrical permit for a full LED lighting replacement and controls upgrade across a 104,207 sq ft campus at 3800 E Hampden Ave — Cherry Hills Village jurisdiction, Englewood mailing address.

Project facts Tap to expand+
Electrical contractor of recordColpitts Electric LLC — permit holder
PermitCherry Hills Village #26-0472 · application #26-0729 · non-residential · approved
Location3800 E Hampden Ave, Cherry Hills Village, CO 80113
Scope426 LED fixtures, lighting controls, and occupancy/vacancy sensors
Crew on site6 — 2 Colpitts electricians, 3 subcontracted electricians, and a working foreman (Melissa Colpitts)
ScheduleCommenced June 2026 · three-month duration · ongoing
Facility104,207 sq ft campus

The scope

Removal and replacement of existing lighting throughout the Children’s Ministry, upstairs offices, fellowship hall, staff kitchen, and conference rooms — replaced with new energy-efficient commercial-grade LED fixtures, plus lighting controls and occupancy/vacancy sensors in every applicable room.

426 fixtures

  • 284 — 2′×4′ LED panels
  • 64 — 8″ can lights
  • 37 — 6″ can lights
  • 20 — 4′ designer strip fixtures
  • 6 — 2′×2′ LED panels
  • 6 — 2′ designer strip fixtures
  • 6 — 8′ designer strip fixtures
  • 3 — vanity fixtures

How we staffed it

Colpitts Electric self-performs with its own electricians and scaled up with three subcontracted electricians to hold the schedule — six on site, run by a working foreman who is also the licensed Master Electrician named on the permit.

Working around a live congregation

The campus serves a congregation of more than 2,000 and stays in use throughout. Children’s Ministry rooms, offices, the fellowship hall, and the staff kitchen all had to come back online between services and weekday programming — so the work is sequenced room by room rather than shutting the building down.

Materials and code

All fixtures, controls, and sensors are new, commercial-grade, and compliant with applicable electrical and energy codes. Fixture models and control systems are approved by the client before procurement, so nothing gets installed that wasn’t signed off first.

The result

Work commenced in June 2026 on a three-month schedule. The client has continued to add scope beyond the original contract — the clearest signal a commercial client can give.
