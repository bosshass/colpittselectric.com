SBA WOSB certified

A federally certified woman-owned electrical contractor.

Colpitts Electric is certified by the U.S. Small Business Administration as both a Women-Owned Small Business (WOSB) and an Economically-Disadvantaged Women-Owned Small Business (EDWOSB), certified since April 5, 2023 — and is certified by the City and County of Denver as a Small Business Enterprise (SBE).

For federal agencies and prime contractors, that means Colpitts Electric is eligible for WOSB and EDWOSB set-aside contracting in designated NAICS codes, and subcontracted work can count toward small business subcontracting goals under an approved plan.

Certification is only part of what we bring to the project. Our value is backed by experienced commercial electrical leadership, jobsite coordination, safety training, and workers who understand that their performance affects the entire construction schedule.

Electrical manpower FAQs

Common questions

What type of companies do you provide electrical manpower for?

Colpitts Electric works with general contractors, electrical contractors, commercial builders, project managers, property owners, and other construction partners throughout the Denver Metro area.

Can you provide electrical workers for only one phase of a project?

Yes. Availability may be structured around a specific project phase, assigned scope, milestone, or period of increased labor demand.

Do you provide electrical supervision?

Supervision requirements should be discussed before scheduling. Tell us whether your team will provide day-to-day direction or whether experienced field leadership is part of the requested scope.

Can Colpitts Electric support WOSB or small business subcontracting goals?

Colpitts Electric holds SBA WOSB and EDWOSB certification, which supports WOSB and EDWOSB set-aside contracting in designated NAICS codes and can count toward small business subcontracting goals. Applicability depends on the solicitation, NAICS code, and funding source, so confirm credit with your contracting officer.

What information do you need to evaluate a manpower request?

Provide the project location, electrical scope, number of workers needed, expected start date, anticipated duration, work schedule, licensing requirements, safety requirements, and whether supervision is needed.
